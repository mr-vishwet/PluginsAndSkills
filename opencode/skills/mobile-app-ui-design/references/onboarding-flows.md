# Onboarding Flows Reference

A field guide to designing onboarding that brings users to value fast. Synthesized from a study of 1,460+ onboarding flows. The core finding: **length is not the point.** The flows that win don't feel like onboarding — they get the user to the moment the product proves its worth.

## The Benchmarks (use these to calibrate, not to copy)

- **Average app = ~25 onboarding screens.** Long is not inherently bad.
- **Longest categories**: finance, health & fitness, education. 7 of the 10 longest flows are finance apps — and several of the longest are also the most successful.
- **Shortest flows skew toward AI products.** When the product speaks for itself, get out of the way.
- **23% of apps personalize during onboarding** — but AI apps only **7%**. AI tools tend to skip up-front questions and let the product learn from usage instead.
- **22% throw a paywall during onboarding.**
- **Web onboarding is ~21% shorter than iOS.** Mobile carries more permission and paywall screens by default — budget for those extra steps on iOS.

> Don't anchor on "keep it short." Anchor on **time-to-value**. A 60-screen flow that feels alive beats a 5-screen flow that feels like a chore.

## The Core Structure: Sign Up → Set Up → Aha Moment

The best flows march toward one thing — the **aha moment**, where the user actually feels the product's value. Map your flow backward from it.

| App | Aha moment |
|-----|-----------|
| Airbnb | Making your first booking |
| Netflix | Finding and watching a show |
| Mobbin | Finding a screen/animation you love and saving it |
| Duolingo | Completing your first lesson |

Design rule: identify your aha moment first, then remove everything between the user and it that isn't earning its place.

## Pattern 1 — Sell the Outcome, Not the Features

The strongest welcome screens don't list features; they show the result.

- **Timehop** — welcome screen is just the product in action (on both mobile and desktop). No feature list.
- **Runkeeper** — an animation on open gives you the feel of the app without reading a single word.
- **Elma** — lets you try the core experience *before* you sign up. (Rare for AI apps — most gate the product behind a signup. Letting users try first is a differentiator.)
- **Superhuman** — a pure copy tweak: turns a boring signup screen into a pitch, with customer logos on the side as social proof.

## Pattern 2 — Feel Human

Small human touches make the product feel made with intention.

- **One Year** — a founder's note in the flow with a handwritten signature and a hand-drawn flower.
- **Tinder** — acknowledges when your birthday is around the corner.
- **Airbnb** — after you list your first space, plays a video from the CEO. A founder's touch placed *at the aha moment*.
- **Basecamp** — a personal note from the CEO right after account creation.

Placement matters: anchor the human moment to the peak (aha) or the end (account created), not buried mid-flow.

## Pattern 3 — Personalize, and Make It Worth Their Time

Don't ask questions for nothing. Every question should visibly change what the user gets.

- **Tide** — download → answer 2 questions → watch it customize recommendations → *then* prompt to sign up. Short and sweet.
- **Headspace** — users arrive with more than one pain point, so let them pick **more than one goal** instead of forcing a single choice. → **+10% free-trial conversion.**
- **Focus Flight** — choose your map style during onboarding. The app feels like *yours* before you've started using it.
- **Dollar Shave Club** — made the quiz copy more conversational. → **+5% subscriptions.** (Copy alone can move the number.)

### Sub-pattern: Show what the answers unlocked

Don't just collect answers — reflect them back as a payoff before the user has even used the product.

- **Endless** — answer 6 questions, then see a tailored result. It already feels like it's going to work.
- **Byte Pal** — after the quiz, builds your personal plan and tells you exactly *when* you'll hit your goal.
- **Brilliant** — shows courses personalized to your responses; the home page lands pre-populated with only the content you asked for.
- **Speak** (language learning) — asks your target language and goals, then: *"In 2 months, you'll be able to communicate while traveling in France,"* plus a simple graph showing speaking beats reading. The steps before this already had you **speaking instead of typing** — the method was demonstrated, not described.

## Pattern 4 — Paywalls That Don't Feel Like Walls

22% of flows paywall during onboarding. Pair it with personalization and earn the moment.

- Quiz + a **one-time offer** to create urgency.
- **Timely** — a full page of social proof *before* the paywall.
- **Focus Flight** — the one-time offer is shaped like a **flight ticket**, and the phone vibrates as it "prints." A paywall that feels delightful.
- **Grammarly** — recommends tailored pricing plans based on quiz answers. → **+~20% plan upgrades.**

The pattern: by the time the paywall appears, the user should already have seen their personalized plan/outcome (Bipul: *"your personal plan is ready, you'll lose weight by [exact date]"* → then the paywall).

## Pattern 5 — Long, but It Doesn't Feel Long

The flows users *love* are often the longest. They hide the length with momentum and delight.

- **Duolingo** — one of the longest: get started → choose language → it learns about you → first lesson → satisfaction of completing it → **then** create an account. ~60 screens before signup — and it doesn't feel long.
- **Bump** — even the loading and verification states are animated. There's always something happening; nothing feels like dead onboarding.
- **Bipul** — 61 screens, rich animations, a lovable raccoon you get to **name** (a virtual pet). Value is reinforced throughout, then the paywall.

Takeaway: invest delight in the screens nobody usually bothers with (loading, verification, transitions). Give the user something to *do* or *name* so the flow feels like play.

## Pattern 6 — Don't Front-Load the Education

Teach in context, step by step, instead of dumping everything up front.

- **Cake Equity** — dry concepts (equity, vesting schedules) made approachable with reassuring copy and **tooltips that explain the impact of each step**, so it feels like someone is guiding you.
- **Real-time field validation** — a password field that checks off requirements as you type removes a reason to get stuck. Same energy: progress indicators and microcopy that make the flow feel effortless.
- **To-do apps** — instead of a blank empty state, show a pre-filled example with **no guided tour, no pop-ups** — just a nudge in the right place.
- **Mural** — replaced pop-ups and banners with a clear **6-step checklist** → **+10% relative one-week retention.** Checklists persist even after the user dismisses the initial flow (search "onboarding checklist" on Mobbin for more).

## Pattern 7 — Prime Before You Ask (Permissions)

Many apps show a **custom screen before the OS notification pop-up** — it significantly improves accept rates.

- **Brilliant** — "I'll remind you to learn so it becomes a long-term habit." Explains the *why* before the system prompt.
- **Center** — goes further and **teases the actual notification** you'll receive if you allow it.

This pre-permission step is a big reason iOS onboarding runs longer than web — plan for it rather than firing the raw OS prompt cold.

## Pattern 8 — Friction Can Be Redistributed

- **House** — split the sign-up form into multiple screens → **+15% conversions.** Friction added in one place (more screens) can remove friction in another (each screen feels trivial).

## Cultural Calibration

Users in Eastern markets tend to be more comfortable with information-heavy interfaces. What reads as clutter to one audience reads as efficient to another. **You can't just copy what worked elsewhere** — calibrate density to the market.

## The Meta-Question: Do You Even Need Onboarding?

Sometimes the best onboarding is none.

- **Mobbin** — design inspiration; the product speaks for itself.
- **AI chat apps** — the first prompt *is* the value. Anything before it is in the way.

For products whose value is immediate, let users get in fast and skip the flow. **It boils down to the product.** Onboarding is a tool to shorten time-to-value — if the product already delivers value in one tap, don't manufacture a flow.

## Onboarding Design Checklist

When designing or reviewing an onboarding flow, ask:

1. **What's the aha moment, and how many screens stand between the user and it?**
2. Is the welcome screen **selling the outcome** (product in action / try-before-signup) or listing features?
3. Does every personalization question **visibly change** what the user gets, and do we **reflect the result back** before they use the product?
4. Is there a **human touch** at the peak or the end?
5. If it's long, is it **alive** — animated loading/verification states, something to do or name?
6. Are we **teaching in context** (tooltips, real-time validation, checklists) instead of front-loading?
7. Is there a **pre-permission screen** before each OS prompt (notifications, location, etc.)?
8. If there's a paywall, has the user **already seen their personalized plan/outcome** first?
9. For an instant-value product — **should there be onboarding at all?**

## Apple HIG Alignment (for native iOS)

Apple's official onboarding guidance reinforces the patterns above — useful authority when designing a native app:

- **Onboarding should be fast, fun, and optional**, and happens *after* launch (it is not the splash screen). The ideal is an app self-explanatory enough to need none — Apple's own "do you even need onboarding?".
- **Teach through interactivity**, or prefer **context-specific tips** (TipKit) surfaced next to the relevant UI over one upfront flow — the in-context version of Pattern 6 ("teach in context").
- **Integrate permission requests into onboarding to show *why* and the benefit** — Apple's explicit endorsement of Pattern 7 (prime before you ask). Otherwise request at first point of use.
- **Let people experience the app before prompting for ratings or purchases** — the HIG version of "earn the paywall."
- **Postpone nonessential setup; ship good defaults.** Make any tutorial skippable, don't re-show it, keep it findable in Help/Settings.

See `apple-hig.md` for the full platform-native ruleset.

## Anti-Patterns Specific to Onboarding

- Listing features on the welcome screen instead of showing the product in action.
- Asking personalization questions that don't change anything the user sees.
- Firing the raw OS permission prompt with no priming screen → tanks accept rates.
- Blank empty states after onboarding with no nudge, example, or checklist.
- Front-loading all education in a wall of tutorial pop-ups.
- Dropping a paywall before the user has seen any personalized value.
- Optimizing for "fewest screens" at the cost of time-to-value or delight.
- Copy-pasting another market's density without cultural calibration.
